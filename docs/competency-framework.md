# Job profile competency framework

Canonical reference for naming, defining, and leveling competencies on job profiles. Use for content design, PM review, and engineering implementation (e.g. Competencies tab).

---

## 1. Competency name

- Short, clear, no fluff.

**Example:** Communication

## 2. Competency definition

- **1–2 sentences max.** Describe what success looks like.

**Format:** The ability to [do what] in order to [business outcome].

**Example:** The ability to clearly convey information and ideas to ensure mutual understanding and effective collaboration.

## 3. Proficiency levels (core of the framework)

- Choose **one of four** levels, aligned with **Career track & Level** where applicable.

**Standard scale:**

| Level | Label        |
| ----- | ------------ |
| 1     | Basic        |
| 2     | Intermediate |
| 3     | Advanced     |
| 4     | Expert       |

## 4. Behavioral indicators

- Write **observable behaviors**, not vague traits.
- Use **bullet points** per level.

### Example ladder (Communication)

**Level 1 – Basic**

- Follows clear instructions
- Communicates simple information clearly
- Asks questions when unsure

**Level 2 – Intermediate**

- Adapts communication to audience
- Shares relevant information proactively
- Participates effectively in discussions

**Level 3 – Advanced**

- Handles complex or sensitive conversations
- Influences others through clear messaging
- Anticipates communication needs

**Level 4 – Expert**

- Shapes communication strategy across teams
- Coaches others on effective communication
- Drives alignment in high-stakes situations

## 5. Do / don’t guidelines

**DO**

- Use action verbs (explains, analyzes, leads)
- Make behaviors observable and measurable
- Keep language consistent across competencies

**DON’T**

- Use vague words (good, strong, nice attitude)
- Combine multiple competencies into one
- Write paragraphs instead of bullet points
